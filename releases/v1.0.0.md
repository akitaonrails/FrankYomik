# v1.0.0

Frank Yomik reads Japanese and Korean comics — and now Japanese novels — in the
original, translating only what you hold the magnifier over. This release is
the point where that works end to end on both clients.

## Japanese text books

Kindle renders a reflowable novel the way it renders manga: one image per page,
no text in the DOM. The `book_furigana` pipeline treats that image as what it
is — typeset prose. Columns are found by their own regularity, read in short
slices, and the readings are drawn into the gutters where vertical Japanese
puts ruby anyway. Nothing on the page itself is redrawn, and gutters that
already carry the publisher's ruby are left alone.

Each Kindle volume remembers its own pipeline, so moving between a novel and a
manga needs no setting changes. A page sent to the wrong pipeline is refused
with an explanation, and the reader corrects itself from what the server
measured rather than asking you to.

## Reading through a magnifier

The page stays in its original language. Press and hold for 200ms and a
magnifier opens under your pointer showing the same page translated or
annotated; release and it is gone. Quick taps still turn pages and webtoons
still scroll.

Getting that right against a real reader took most of this release:

- A held peek takes its events away from the reader, so the page no longer
  drags underneath the lens.
- Kindle begins selecting from the press itself, so the press is taken and a
  tap handed back — once, not twice — and releasing a peek no longer opens the
  highlight menu.
- A render is checked against the page it is bound to before it is shown, and
  bound to the element it was captured from rather than the one that scores
  best. Neighbouring pages are the same size and in the same place; only
  identity tells them apart.
- Captures wait for the page to decode, and for a reflowable book to finish
  paginating, so what is translated is the page you are looking at.

A small dot in the corner says where a page is — amber while it works, green
when it can be peeked, red when it could not be translated.

## Under the hood

- Server dependencies are bounded at their majors; an unpinned rebuild had
  moved opencv across a major version unnoticed.
- Page retention is bounded by bytes as well as pages, since a prose render is
  much larger than a manga one.
- The extension's tests now include a real headless Chromium, because the
  questions that mattered most — whether the reader's own handlers still fire
  during a peek, and whether a page and its translation measure as the same
  page — cannot be answered outside a browser.
